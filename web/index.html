How a circle works

A ROSCA is one of the oldest savings habits in the world. Ootle Circle puts its rules on-chain, so no one has to trust the organiser with the money.

1. Create a circle

Set a name, a fixed contribution, a round length, and how many members the circle holds. The circle opens for joining.

2. Members join

Each member joins with the account that will receive their payout. When the circle fills, round one starts.

3. Contribute each round

Every member pays exactly the contribution, once per round, inside the round window. Wrong amounts and double payments are rejected.

4. One member takes the pot

When the last member pays, the whole pot goes to the member whose turn it is. After every member has taken the pot once, the circle closes.

What the contract cannot do

It cannot force a member to pay, and it cannot see whether someone meant to pay. If a round stalls because someone stops paying, the only remedy is the creator closing the circle early, which refunds everyone who paid that round. That is the same trust model as a real-world savings circle, and it is stated here on purpose.

      The contract

      A Rust template for Tari Ootle, compiled to WASM. The same rules you just tried in the simulator, enforced on-chain.

      What is on-chain

      • Circle state: name, contribution, round length, member list, pot vault, current round, status.
      • One fixed contribution per member per round, enforced exactly.
      • Automatic payout to the member whose turn it is, the moment the last member pays.
      • Round windows: late contributions are rejected, so a round cannot drag forever.
      • Early close refunds the current round; cancel closes a circle that never fills.
      • Every payout goes to an account that was verified to exist when the member joined.

      Engine tests

      23 passed, 0 failed - engine tests against the real Ootle engine, in-process.

      • Full rotation: three members, three rounds, correct payouts, everyone whole at the end. A two-member rotation completes too.
      • Payout only when everyone has paid; pot holds the partial round until then.
      • Wrong amount, double payment, non-member payment, and wrong-resource payment all rejected.
      • Join twice, join a full circle, pay before active, and pay after the round deadline all rejected. Join at the exact fill deadline rejected; pay at the exact round deadline allowed.
      • Early close refunds the current round, including with no payers and after the deadline passes; only the creator can close early. Cancel and close-early rejected in the wrong state.
      • Cancel after the join window; cancel before it is rejected; bad create parameters rejected; public view getters report the correct state.

      Questions

      Is this real money?

      No. The demo on this page is a simulation with play TARI. The contract itself accepts any fungible resource, and on-chain it would hold real TARI. Nothing on this page moves real funds.

      What happens if someone stops paying?

      The round stalls: no payout happens until everyone has paid. The creator can close the circle early, which refunds everyone who paid that round. Members who already took a payout keep it, because they paid their share in earlier rounds.

      Does anyone take a fee?

      No. The contract takes no fee. On-chain, the only cost is the network fee for each transaction, paid to Tari validators.

      Is the contract audited?

      No. It is unaudited. It is tested against the real Ootle engine, but that is not an audit. Do not put money you cannot afford to lose into any unaudited contract, including this one.

      Why a fixed contribution?

      A ROSCA works because everyone pays the same amount. A variable amount would let one member pay less and still take the full pot, which breaks the circle. The contract rejects any payment that is not exactly the contribution.
