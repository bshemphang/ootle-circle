The idea behind the circle

Learn how a ROSCA works

A rotating savings circle is one of the oldest savings habits in the world. Follow the five steps of one circle's life, from creation to close.

  1. 1. Create
  2. 2. Join
  3. 3. Pay
  4. 4. Payout
  5. 5. Close
STEP 1 / CREATE

One person starts the circle

The creator sets the terms: a name, a fixed contribution, a round length, and how many members the circle holds. In the example, that is Shillong Friends Circle, 50 TARI a round, 5 members, rounds of 10 epochs. The circle opens for joining, and the creator becomes member 1.

1 of 5 slots filled. The circle is open for joining.

STEP 2 / JOIN

Members join with their payout account

Each member joins with the account that will receive their payout. The contract checks the account exists before accepting the join. When the circle fills, round 1 starts. A circle that never fills can be cancelled after its join window passes.

5 of 5 slots filled. Round 1 starts now.

You receive the full pot once, and pay into it every round.
STEP 3 / PAY

Everyone pays the same, every round

Every member pays exactly the contribution, once per round, inside the round window. In the example that is 50 TARI each, so the pot reaches 250 TARI. Wrong amounts, double payments, and late payments are rejected by the contract, not by a person.

4 of 5 paid. The pot holds 200 TARI until the last member pays.

STEP 4 / PAYOUT

The pot goes to the member whose turn it is

When the last member pays, the whole pot goes to the member whose turn it is. There is no claim call and no waiting for an organiser. Round 1 pays Asha, round 2 pays Boris, round 3 pays Chloe, round 4 pays Dev, round 5 pays you. The turn order is the join order.

STEP 5 / CLOSE

After every member takes the pot once, the circle closes

Five rounds, five payouts, everyone whole. Each member paid 250 TARI in total and took 250 TARI once. If a round stalls because someone stops paying, the creator can close early and refund everyone who paid that round. That is the same trust model as a real-world savings circle.

No interest, no fee, no middleman. The contract holds the pot and follows the rotation.

Glossary

ROSCA
A rotating savings and credit association. A group that saves together and takes turns receiving the pooled money.
Pot
The total collected in one round. In the example, 5 members x 50 TARI = 250 TARI.
Round
One cycle of contributions ending in one payout. A 5-member circle has 5 rounds.
Epoch
A unit of time on Tari. Round lengths and join windows are measured in epochs.
Vault
The contract's holding for the pot. Only the contract can release it, and only as a full payout to the member whose turn it is.

Circle vs bank vs lending

A circle is not a replacement for a bank. It does one thing well and several things not at all. Here is the honest comparison.

Rotating circleBank savingsShort loan
Do you earn interest?NoYes, smallNo, you pay it
Do you pay a fee?NoSometimesYes, interest and fees
Can you take a lump sum early?Yes, on your turnOnly what you savedYes, at once
Who holds the money?The contractThe bankThe lender
What if someone stops paying?The round stallsNothing happens to youCollections and penalties
Credit check?NoFor an account, rarelyYes
Best forForced saving with friendsSafe, slow savingUrgent lump sum

The circle wins on discipline and on cost. It loses on flexibility: it cannot help a member who needs money outside their turn, and it cannot protect the group from a member who stops paying.

Questions

Is this real money?

No. The demo on this site is a simulation with play TARI. The contract itself accepts any fungible resource, and on-chain it would hold real TARI. Nothing on this site moves real funds.

What happens if someone stops paying?

The round stalls: no payout happens until everyone has paid. The creator can close the circle early, which refunds everyone who paid that round. Members who already took a payout keep it, because they paid their share in earlier rounds.

Does anyone take a fee?

No. The contract takes no fee. On-chain, the only cost is the network fee for each transaction, paid to Tari validators.

Is the contract audited?

No. It is unaudited. It is tested against the real Ootle engine, but that is not an audit. Do not put money you cannot afford to lose into any unaudited contract, including this one.

Why a fixed contribution?

A ROSCA works because everyone pays the same amount. A variable amount would let one member pay less and still take the full pot, which breaks the circle. The contract rejects any payment that is not exactly the contribution.

Who goes first?

The rotation order is the order members joined. Member 1 takes the pot in round 1, member 2 in round 2, and so on. There is no bidding and no lottery in this contract.

Can a circle have more than 8 members?

Not in this contract. The template caps a circle at 8 members, set at creation. A bigger circle means a longer wait for the last member and a bigger pot, and the cap keeps the circle short enough that the wait stays reasonable.
